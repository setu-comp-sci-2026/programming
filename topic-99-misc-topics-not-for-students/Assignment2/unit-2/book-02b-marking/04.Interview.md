# 04. Interview 

You **may** be invited for an interview. This is to ascertain that you have a good understanding of the submitted project and are able to answer questions about it.

## Interview
You may be asked to present your work in person at the interview. This will involve explaining your submission, how it works, and a question and answer session where we will ask you questions about any part of your submissions. 

The date of the interview will be published closer to the time. The mark of the interview will be used as a multiplier for the final mark of the assignment. 

**If** you are invited for an interview:
 - attendance is mandatory and failure to attend will leave to 0% mark (which as multiplier will lead to an overall final mark of 0%).
    
**(else) If you are not** invited for an interview:
- the mark for the interview will be 100% (no multiplier).  

## During the interview, you may be asked (for instance) to demonstrate some of the following:

### Run your expert system
Run your expert system and reach a recommendation.

### Reasoning

Explain why the recommendation was produced.

### Knowledge Base

Show how questions, rules and recommendations are represented.

### CRUD

Add, view, edit and delete knowledge.

### Persistence

Make a change, save it, restart the program and demonstrate that the change remains.

### Object-Oriented Design

Explain important classes and objects.

### Explain code
Explain any part of the code, how it works, how it is used within the project. 


### Unseen Change
You may be given a small **unseen change to the expert knowledge**.
For example:

> Add another question.

> Add another recommendation.

> Modify an existing rule.

> Change a boundary value.

> Add another condition to a rule.

You should be able to make an appropriate change to the Knowledge Base and demonstrate its effect.

For suitable changes to expert knowledge, you should **not normally need to rewrite the inference engine**.

This is an important test of the design of your system.
